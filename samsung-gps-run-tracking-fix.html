Why your Samsung phone stops tracking your run — and how to fix it

Gaps in your route, straight lines across the map, a 5 km run logged as 3 km? It’s almost never the app. Here’s the real cause and the exact fix.

You finish a run, open your tracker, and the route looks wrong: a long straight line where a bend should be, a chunk of distance missing, or pace that jumps around. If you’re on a Samsung Galaxy — or a Xiaomi, OnePlus, Oppo or Huawei — there’s a good chance your phone, not your app, cut the recording short.

What’s actually happening

Modern Android phones work hard to save battery. When your screen turns off, the system can put background apps to sleep — pausing their access to GPS even while you’re still moving. This is called Doze in stock Android, and phone makers layer their own, more aggressive versions on top:

  • Samsung — “Sleeping apps” and “Deep sleeping apps,” plus adaptive battery.
  • Xiaomi (MIUI/HyperOS) — aggressive background limits and “Battery saver” restrictions.
  • OnePlus / Oppo (OxygenOS/ColorOS) — “Sleep standby optimization” and deep optimization.

When the tracker is put to sleep mid-run, it stops getting location points. Your app later draws a straight line between the last point before the freeze and the first point after it — which is why you see missing distance and odd pace. The run wasn’t recorded wrong; your phone paused the recorder.

Quick test: If your route looks fine when you run with the screen on the whole time, but has gaps when the screen is off, battery optimization is almost certainly the cause.

The fix on a Samsung Galaxy

You need to tell Android to leave your tracking app alone. On a Samsung phone (One UI), do all three of these:

1. Set the app to “Unrestricted” battery use

  1. Open Settings → Apps and tap your tracking app.
  2. Tap Battery.
  3. Choose Unrestricted (not “Optimised” or “Restricted”).

2. Remove it from “Sleeping apps”

  1. Open Settings → Battery → Background usage limits.
  2. Check the Sleeping apps and Deep sleeping apps lists — if your tracker is there, remove it.
  3. Turn off “Put unused apps to sleep” if you want to be certain.

3. Keep location on “Allow all the time” and precise

  1. In the app’s info screen, tap Permissions → Location.
  2. Make sure precise location is on.
  3. Keep your phone’s Location and GPS turned on before you start.

Xiaomi: Settings → Apps → your app → Battery saver → No restrictions, and turn on Autostart.
OnePlus/Oppo: Settings → Battery → Battery optimization → set the app to Don’t optimize, and disable Sleep standby optimization.

Frequently asked

Will turning off battery optimization drain my battery?

Only while you’re actually recording a run. A GPS tracker you’ve set to “Unrestricted” still sleeps normally when you’re not using it — it just isn’t frozen mid-activity. The battery cost of a 30–60 minute run is small.

Why does the map show a straight line across part of my run?

That straight line is your app connecting the last GPS point before the phone paused tracking to the first point after it woke up. Fixing battery optimization stops the gaps, and the line.

I did all this and it still happens. What now?

A few phones reset these settings after an update, so re-check them. It can also be genuine signal loss in tunnels, dense cities or under heavy tree cover. Vigor tells you which of the two caused a gap, so you’re not guessing.
