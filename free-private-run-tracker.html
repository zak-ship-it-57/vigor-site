Private running

The best free run tracker keeps your data on your phone

Most running apps copy every route you run to their servers and make you sign up first. You don’t have to give that away to track a run.

A running log is a map of your life: where you live, when you leave the house, the quiet roads you run alone, the route to work. Most popular trackers upload all of it to the cloud and tie it to an account. If you just want to see your distance, pace and route, there’s a simpler, more private way to do it.

What “private” actually means for a run tracker

“Free” and “private” often pull in opposite directions — plenty of free apps pay for themselves by collecting and sharing data. A genuinely private run tracker is one where:

  • Your routes stay on your device. Location is used to measure your run, then saved on the phone — not streamed to a company’s servers by default.
  • There’s no account to create. No email, no social login, nothing linking your runs to a profile.
  • There are no trackers or ad IDs. Nothing in the background profiling you or selling an audience.
  • If it does sync, it’s encrypted end to end — so even the sync server can’t read your routes.
  • It works offline. A tracker that records fine in airplane mode isn’t quietly phoning home.

Quick way to check any app: open its Play Store listing and read the Data safety section. It has to declare what it collects and whether it’s shared. “No data collected” is the gold standard for a tracker.

Switching from a cloud tracker like Strava?

Big social fitness apps are great if you want leaderboards and a feed. But a lot of runners don’t — they want their distance, their route and their history, without the account, the subscription prompts or the data collection. If that’s you, a lightweight on-device tracker covers the essentials:

  • GPS distance, pace and splits, with your route on a map.
  • Your full history and personal records, kept on the phone.
  • Export your activities as standard files (GPX/FIT) whenever you want — your data is never locked in.

You get the numbers that matter and keep ownership of the data behind them.

Frequently asked

Is a free run tracker any good, or is it free for a reason?

Both can be true, which is why the Data safety section matters. An app that declares “no data collected,” has no account and no ads isn’t monetising your runs — it’s simply a tracker. Vigor is free while it’s new, with a paid Pro tier planned later for extras, not for the basics.

Can I move my history to another app later?

Yes. A tracker that respects your data lets you export activities as standard GPX or FIT files. Vigor does, so you can back them up or import them elsewhere — nothing is locked in.

If my runs stay on my phone, what happens if I lose it?

Keep a backup. You can export your activities, or turn on Vigor’s end-to-end encrypted sync to keep a copy on another phone. Because sync is encrypted with a key only your devices hold, the server storing it still can’t read your routes.

Does keeping data on-device mean no maps?

No. Vigor still shows your route on a map and has a heatmap of everywhere you’ve been. The difference is that your activities are stored on the phone rather than uploaded to a company’s servers by default.
